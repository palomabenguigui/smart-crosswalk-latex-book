# vanhouten2007 – Van Houten, Ellis & Kim (2007), TRR 2002, 78–83

**Read:** Abstract (OpenAlex).

## Key findings (own words)
- Two midblock signalised crosswalks in Miami-Dade County, Florida. The **minimum vehicle green** (and so the pedestrian wait)
  was varied between **30 and 120 s**.
- **Compliance fell as the minimum green (wait) grew.** It fell faster at the one-way site with lower traffic.
- Longer minimum greens also left more pedestrians stranded at the centre line.

## Why it matters
- Closest to an **experiment** on our core lever: the signal timing was manipulated, not just observed across sites.
  It is the key causal support for the claim that a shorter pedestrian wait improves compliance (ch. 4, ch. 6 A2).
- It also links to the safe-limits question. The variable manipulated *is* the vehicle minimum green (see `fhwa2008stm`).
