# wickramasinghe2020 – Wickramasinghe & Ganegoda (2020), Proc. ICTer 2020 (IEEE), 172–177

**Read:** Abstract (OpenAlex, p. 172). Full text not read.

## Key findings (own words)
- Sri Lankan context: current crossings use fixed timers or push buttons. The authors propose camera-based image
  processing to detect, track and count pedestrians, estimate their **waiting time** and predict their path.
- Reported performance: a Haar-cascade detector with about 50% precision and about 86% recall; counting accuracy of 72%;
  an average waiting-time error of 0.714 (the unit is not stated in the abstract: check); path prediction of about 78% correct.

## What it does not show
- No field safety outcome (no effect on violations). It is a prototype accuracy study using an older detector method.

## How the book can use it
- Ch. 5/7: shows that **counting and measuring waiting time at the curb** is technically feasible with a camera.
  Ch. 9: the modest precision supports a risk entry on detection accuracy. Ch. 6: the camera vs radar/infrared alternative.
- Privacy: camera-based sensing motivates the privacy-by-design section (ch. 7).
