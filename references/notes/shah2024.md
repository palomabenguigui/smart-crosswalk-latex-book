# shah2024 – Shah & Pradhananga (2024), Transportation Research Interdisciplinary Perspectives 24, 101035

**Read:** Abstract only. The article is open access, but ScienceDirect blocked automated access.

## Key findings (own words, from the abstract)
- 1,790 pedestrians at signalised crosswalks in Kathmandu, analysed with binary logit and Kaplan–Meier survival.
- Significant predictors include the **number of pedestrians waiting**, group crossing, crosswalk length, perceived traffic
  and the **remaining red time until green**.
- About 35% crossed on red at once, without waiting at all.
- Pedestrians tended to wait for green when the **remaining red was under about 50 s**. With **more than about 100 s** left,
  they tended to cross almost immediately.

## Use
- ch. 2/4: a concrete waiting-tolerance threshold to support the adaptive-red goal (keep the remaining wait short).
  Note the context: a lower-middle-income country with different enforcement.
- A1 nuance: if pedestrians react to *remaining* red, a red countdown that reveals a long remaining wait could trigger early crossing.
