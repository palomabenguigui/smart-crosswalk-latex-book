# zhou2009 – Zhou, Horrey & Yu (2009), Accident Analysis & Prevention 41(3), 491–497

**Read:** Abstract (PubMed PMID 19393798, p. 491). Full text not read.

## Key findings (own words)
- Survey of 426 respondents in China, built on the theory of planned behaviour plus a social-conformity scale.
- Two scenarios: crossing on red when **others are crossing** and crossing when others are not.
- Respondents said they were more likely to cross when others crossed. People high in conformity reported stronger
  intentions to cross in both scenarios.
- The model explained 36% (non-conformity scenario) and 48% (conformity scenario) of the variance in intention.
  Attitude, subjective norm, perceived control and perceived risk predicted intention in both.
- The authors suggest campaigns stating that crossing against the signal **with a group** is still unsafe and illegal.

## Limits
- Self-reported intentions, not observed behaviour, in one cultural setting.

## How the book can use it
- Ch. 4 (social influence): survey-level support for the group effect. Ch. 7 (campaign): message framing.
