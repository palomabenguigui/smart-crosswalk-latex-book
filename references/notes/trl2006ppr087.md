# trl2006ppr087 – Wood, Crabtree & Gutteridge (2006), TRL PPR087, *Pedestrian and vehicular detectors for traffic management and control*

**Read:** Full text (grey literature, written for the UK Department for Transport; not peer-reviewed).

## Content (own words)
- Reviews detector technologies for UK signal control. Pedestrian types: passive and active infrared (§2), microwave (§3),
  image processing/video (§4: false negatives and positives, changing and low light, set-up, maintenance), sub-surface pressure mats (§5),
  and pedestrian radar for on-crossing and kerbside use (§6).
- The summary table (p. 11) compares each technology's objective, mounting, advantages and disadvantages. For example:
  - Video can potentially **count** pedestrians but suffers from false detections and low light.
  - Microwave is an established, low-cost product but struggles with very slow pedestrians and alignment.
  - Mats have a well-defined zone, but pedestrians may not stand on them.
  - Radar was "not yet fully developed" (in 2006).

## Use
- ch. 5: the product-class comparison (technical, not marketing) for curb sensing. ch. 6: a criteria source for the decision matrix.
- ch. 9: false and missed detection risks per technology.
