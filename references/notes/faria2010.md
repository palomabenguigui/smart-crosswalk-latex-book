# faria2010 – Faria, Krause & Krause (2010), Behavioral Ecology 21(6), 1236–1242

**Read:** Abstract (OpenAlex, p. 1236). Full text not read.

## Key findings (own words)
- Video-based observational study of who crosses first in groups of waiting pedestrians, compared with a null model
  in which nobody follows anybody.
- People standing next to someone who starts crossing tend to go next. The authors estimate a person is roughly
  1.5 to 2.5 times as likely to cross once a neighbour has started.
- Men followed others more than women did.
- Some people started, then stepped back to the curb. These were more often in groups and started later, which the
  authors read as wrong timing decisions caused by copying others.
- Conclusion: the small time saving from following others comes with more risk.

## How the book can use it
- Ch. 4: quantitative evidence for imitation at the curb (a "leader" effect). It complements `rosenbloom2009`,
  where people in groups crossed on red **less**. The book should discuss this tension: the group can restrain or trigger crossing,
  depending on whether the first mover waits. Ch. 7: the sensing layer could detect a "first mover" event.
