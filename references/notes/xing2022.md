# xing2022 – Xing, Zhang, Cheng & Zu (2022), IJERPH 19(21), 14420 (open access)

**Read:** Full text (PMC9654891). The article has no journal page numbers, so cite sections.

## Key findings (own words)
- 16 signalised intersections in Suzhou, China. Video surveys of about 4,590 pedestrians, of whom 881 crossed on red
  (the abstract says 4,586 and §4.1 says 4,589: a small internal inconsistency).
- Site level (§4.2–4.3, Tables 2–3): the violation rate was significantly related to crossing distance, traffic volume,
  **length of red** and crosswalk type. **Longer red went with more violations** (coefficient +2.51, p = 0.028).
  Whether a **red-light countdown** was installed was **not** significantly related to the violation rate (§4.2).
- Survival analysis (§4.4): the share of people still waiting falls fastest **in the first 10 s** of waiting.
  Violations cluster at the start of red. The survival curves compare *sites* with different red lengths, so they
  show selection effects, not the effect of shortening red at one site.

## Limits
- Correlational, only 16 sites, and a single crosswalk per intersection. The temporal and site-level results point in different directions
  and must not be over-read.

## Chapters
ch. 2 (open-access numbers), ch. 4 (waiting time and red length; countdown not significant, which bears on A1),
ch. 7 (violations cluster early in red, which affects how the adaptive algorithm is timed).
