# lenne2007 – Lenné, Corben & Stephan (2007), Accident Analysis & Prevention 39(4), 751–756

**Read:** Abstract (PubMed PMID 17196918).

## Key findings (own words)
- "Dwell-on-Red": during high-risk periods, an intersection rests on red for all vehicle approaches when no vehicle is detected,
  so drivers approach more slowly. The target is alcohol-affected pedestrians (Melbourne, Australia).
- Before/after with a control site: mean speed fell by **3.9 km/h (9%)** 30 m upstream and by **11.0 km/h (28%)** at the stop line,
  with far fewer vehicles at speeds dangerous to pedestrians.

## Use
- ch. 4/6 (B2 evidence): a **detector-driven, time-of-day signal mode** that changes the default state to protect pedestrians.
  It is a precedent for adaptive operation and for the flagship idea of adjusting red "at certain times of the day" (`rosenbloom2009` §4.5).
- Note: it measures vehicle speed, not pedestrian violations.
