# fu2016 – Fu & Zou (2016), Accident Analysis & Prevention 94, 73–79

**Read:** Abstract (PubMed PMID 27261555, p. 73). Full text not read.

## Key findings (own words)
- Compares two school intersections in Jinan, China: one with countdown heads, one without. Subjects: children.
- A countdown shown **during the red ("Red Man") phase** went with *more* violations and more running across among children,
  and those violators had more conflicts with vehicles.
- On the positive side, the countdown helped children finish crossing before red began, so fewer were caught mid-crosswalk.
- No difference between the two sites for children starting during flashing green.
- Children crossing **alone** violated more than children with companions. This matches the social-control finding in `rosenbloom2009`.
- Boys ran more than girls. Other behaviours showed no gender difference.
- The authors recommend **switching off the countdown near the end of the red phase**.

## Limits
- Only two sites, cross-sectional comparison (not before/after), and children only.

## How the book can use it
- Ch. 4: key **counter-evidence**. A red-phase countdown can encourage early starts. Ch. 6/7: design rule:
  hide the last seconds of the red countdown. Ch. 9: risk "countdown on red increases early starts", with this mitigation.
