# eccles2004 – Eccles, Tao & Mangum (2004), TRR 1878, 36–41

**Read:** Abstract (OpenAlex; first page of the article, p. 36). Full text not read.

## Key findings (own words)
- Before/after field evaluation of countdown heads at five intersections in Montgomery County, Maryland (US).
- Measures: the signal phase at which pedestrians stepped off the curb, pedestrians still in the road when
  conflicting traffic got green, pedestrian–vehicle conflicts, and vehicle approach speed.
- Overall, the countdown did **not** make pedestrian behaviour worse. Results by crosswalk were mixed:
  6 of 20 crosswalks showed significantly more pedestrians starting on WALK, while 2 of 20 showed significantly fewer.
- Conflicts between pedestrians and vehicles dropped significantly at four of the intersections where they were observed.
- Approach speeds during the flashing DON'T WALK interval did not change.
- A survey showed pedestrians noticed and understood the countdown.

## What it does not show
- It tested the **US-style countdown during the clearance interval**, not a countdown during the red (DON'T WALK) phase.
  It says nothing directly about red-light waiting.

## How the book can use it
- Ch. 4: evidence that countdown displays are understood and do not harm safety. Ch. 6: the baseline "clearance-countdown" alternative.
